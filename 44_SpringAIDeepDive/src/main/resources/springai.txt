--- SECTION 1: SPRING AI CORE & RAG ---
Spring AI is an application framework designed to simplify the integration of Generative AI into Java applications.
It applies familiar Spring design principles, such as portability and modularity. The framework provides a unified
abstraction layer for interacting with multiple AI providers like OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google Vertex AI.
A key feature is the Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline, which allows developers to connect their
enterprise data to AI models. The RAG workflow in Spring AI includes the DocumentReader for ingestion,
DocumentTransformer for chunking, and VectorStore for storage. Common advisors like the QuestionAnswerAdvisor
automate the retrieval of context based on user queries.

--- SECTION 2: FUNCTION CALLING & AGENTS ---
Spring AI supports "Tool Calling" (also known as Function Calling). This allows developers to define a
standard java.util.Function as a @Bean, which the AI model can then trigger to perform real-time actions,
such as checking weather data or processing a payment. The framework handles the JSON schema generation
and the mapping of the model's request to the Java function. Recent updates also introduced agentic
patterns, including the A2A (Agent-to-Agent) protocol, which is a vendor-neutral standard built on
HTTP and JSON-RPC for inter-agent communication and discovery.

--- SECTION 3: OBSERVABILITY & ACTUATOR ---
Spring AI leverages the Spring Boot Actuator to provide deep observability into AI operations. It provides
metrics and tracing for ChatClient, ChatModel, and VectorStore. By adding the 'spring-boot-starter-actuator'
dependency, developers can track low-cardinality keys in metrics and high-cardinality keys in traces.
Observability currently covers model latency and token usage, though support for tracing specific
Function Calling branches is an area of active development.

--- SECTION 4: SPRING BOOT & CLOUD CONTEXT (DISTRACTORS) ---
While Spring AI focuses on models, Spring Boot 3.4 introduced general enhancements such as improved
virtual thread support and refined observability for JDBC. Spring Cloud continues to provide tools
for distributed systems, including Service Discovery via Netflix Eureka and Configuration Management
via Spring Cloud Config. These features are distinct from the AI-specific abstractions but follow
the same "Starters" and "Autoconfiguration" patterns.

--- SECTION 5: NEW YORK CHEESECAKE INGREDIENTS ---
A classic New York cheesecake requires specific high-fat ingredients for its signature dense
texture. You will need 4 packages (8 oz each) of full-fat cream cheese at room temperature,
1.5 cups of granulated sugar, and 4 large eggs. For the graham cracker crust, combine
1.5 cups of crumbs with 6 tablespoons of melted unsalted butter and 2 tablespoons of sugar.
The inclusion of 1 cup of heavy cream or sour cream is essential for the creamy mouthfeel
characteristic of the Junior's style cheesecake.

--- SECTION 6: BAKING TECHNIQUE & TEMPERATURE ---
The secret to a crack-free cheesecake is the "Bain-Marie" or water bath method. Wrap the
outside of your springform pan in three layers of heavy-duty aluminum foil to prevent leaks.
Place the pan inside a larger roasting pan filled with 1 inch of boiling water. Bake at
325°F (163°C) for approximately 75–90 minutes. Once the edges are set but the center still
has a slight "jiggle," turn off the oven and crack the door open. Let it cool inside the
oven for one hour to prevent sudden temperature drops that cause surface cracking.

--- SECTION 7: CHILLING AND SERVING ---
After the oven cooling phase, remove the cheesecake and let it reach room temperature on
a wire rack. It must be refrigerated for at least 12 hours (preferably 24) before slicing.
For clean slices, use a thin-bladed knife dipped in hot water and wiped dry between every
single cut. Serve plain or with a simple macerated strawberry topping.

--- SECTION 8: LANGCHAIN EXPRESSION LANGUAGE (LCEL) ---
LangChain is a popular Python and JavaScript framework for building LLM applications.
The core of the framework is LCEL (LangChain Expression Language), a declarative
way to compose chains together. Unlike Spring AI's ChatClient, LCEL uses a
"pipe" operator (|) to link prompts, models, and output parsers. It supports
first-class primitives for batching, streaming, and async operations.

--- SECTION 9: LANGGRAPH AND AGENTS ---
LangGraph is a library within the LangChain ecosystem designed for building
stateful, multi-actor applications with LLMs. It represents agentic workflows
as cyclic graphs, allowing for loops that are difficult to implement in
standard linear chains. While Spring AI uses the A2A protocol for agents,
LangGraph relies on a persistent 'State' object that is passed between nodes
in the graph to maintain memory and context across turns.

--- SECTION 10: DOCUMENT LOADERS AND SPLITTERS ---
LangChain provides a vast library of Document Loaders to fetch data from
sources like Slack, Notion, and Google Drive. Once loaded, text is processed
using 'RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter', which attempts to split text at
natural boundaries like double newlines or spaces. This is the equivalent
of Spring AI's DocumentTransformer but offers different strategies for
handling overlapping chunks in the vector store.
